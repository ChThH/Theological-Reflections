# Speaker’s Outline: The Dormition and Our Christian Death
## Introduction
* Human experience: We all have an unknown expiration date.
* Psalm 38 meditation on fleeting life.
* Liturgy prayer for a peaceful, blameless Christian death.
* Immediately after, we call on the Theotokos, Mary.
* Dormition shows us how to approach death — a new beginning in Christ.

## The Tradition of the Dormition
* Brief adapted story from Dormition Matins:
* Mary’s peaceful death and Apostles gathering.
* Absence of Apostle Thomas, his arrival and empty tomb.
* Mary’s appearance promising to always be with us.

## The Theotokos, Our Great Example
* Mary’s lifelong “Let it be to me according to your word.”
* Full cooperation with God from youth to death.
* Death as universal — Mary’s death resonates with Paschal themes.
* Remembering death aids righteous living (Wisdom of Sirach).
* We imitate Mary pointing us to Jesus, the Way.

## The Theotokos, Our Great Protection
* The Christian death we seek requires daily dying to self and sin.
* Sins’ pleasures are hollow; Resurrection life is true joy.
* Begin by:
  * Daily prayer
  * Scripture reading
  * Compassion
  * Frequent confession
  * Self-denial is built moment-by-moment, not instantaneously.
* Mary as our indefatigable ally:
  * Helps banish demons, calm passions.
  * Quotes from Paraklesis prayer.
* What truly profits us?
  * St Paul (Romans 5): rejoicing even in suffering — it builds hope and character.
  *St James: count trials as joy; testing produces steadfastness.
* Mary suffered immensely with Christ—ask her aid, she is close.
* Struggle and suffering participate in God’s work → true joy.
* The tomb is a transition to life (Vespers hymn).
* Fr John Behr: death is falling asleep to arise with Christ — a beginning.

## Conclusion
* Psalm 38: Our hope is in the Lord.
* Funeral hymns remind us of our return to earth and hope to be refashioned.
* Faithful in Christ, by grace, we will have a Christian ending.
* Death leads to being reshaped by God, like Adam from dust.
